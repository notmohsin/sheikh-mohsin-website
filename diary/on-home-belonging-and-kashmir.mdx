The idea of home needs no introduction, in this life or any that follow, as simple as it may sound. The ideas of home and belonging are often found in the same sentence, but are they really two sides of the same coin, or two different peaks on different planets? It is a question that I have set out to answer, for now.

I had to redefine home and belonging twice before I reached my twenties. I do not take pride in it, but it is simply how life panned out for me. Reflections on the meaning and idea of home are not new to me; they have been a quiet part of my life, and at times caused torments throughout the soul and body all at once. At other times, I am content, not overjoyed. I am here; I am present; or, in a cliché sense, I am.

In this series, I have set out to ask questions, and maybe attempt to answer them as well. This is something that I have done a lot beforehand, in my own capacity, but now, for the first time in my life, I am turning outward, to the world, in the hope that I may find answers to the questions that have tormented me for most of my thinking life.

Perhaps I have spent too much time asking these questions in my own head. Perhaps there is only so much that one can understand by looking inward for so long. So, for now, I am going to put them down, one by one, and see where they take me. I do not know how they might be answered, or whether they can be answered at all, but here I am, trying something that I have not done before.

1. What does “Home” even mean? What does it mean for me?
2. Do I have a “Home”, and where is it? If I do not, how does one find one for themselves?
3. What does it mean to belong to a place, or to another soul, and to find comfort within that belonging?
4. Can I ever return to the home that I left? Is it even there, and am I here or there?
5. Can I be homesick and home-averse at the same time?

The world that I am growing up in is changing rapidly; it always has, and maybe it is cliché to say that this time it is “different”, but change has always been different since the beginning of time till its end. The _Kashmir_ that I once knew and lived in is gone forever, into the abyss called time. The streets and paths, concreted, maybe, but they are there. The mountains certainly are, but not much can be said about the ice and the snow that once graced them. Perhaps some of the people whom I knew and who knew me still remain, but the _Kashmir_ I long for, I think, only remains in my memories from a time gone by. So even if I wanted to return with all my heart, I am not sure I would find the same place, the same people, or perhaps even the same feeling that once made it home. And this leaves me with a question that I cannot quite escape: when we long to return home, are we really longing to return to a place, or to a moment that has already passed us by?

_Kashmir_, in fact, has changed for better or for worse is an argument for another time and maybe for one of our “political leaders” to answer. But places are not the only things that change. The people who inhabit them do too. The people of _Kashmir_ have changed, as have I. I have loved them with all my being, and I still do, but at times I find myself questioning myself, and us, about the actions we have taken, both individually and collectively, and about the words we have spoken. Have we ever stopped to ask ourselves what their consequences might be? Perhaps we have not had the luxury of doing so. Perhaps our reality, defined by transgressions of one kind or another, has rarely given us the time, the distance, or even the peace to look inward and ask.

And perhaps I am as guilty as I have accused others to be. Perhaps it is easier for me and for humans to romanticise the past than to interrogate. Easier to remember the snow, the mountains, the people, the roads, the apple trees, the cherries, the warmth of a place that I once called home, than to ask what home was actually like. Memory has a strange and a blissful habit of choosing what it wants to preserve. I wonder how much of home I remember is home itself and how much of it is a story constructed from fragments of a memory that I do not hold in its entirety.

Perhaps that, too, is a part of what makes home complicated for me and for so many like myself, torn between a construct we once knew and a home that we do not recognise. Between what was and what is. Between what we remember and what remains. Between what we once were and what we are, between a place that remains and the feelings that do not.

Maybe home is not a place at all, at least not entirely. Maybe it is a point where a place, a time, a memory and a feeling briefly met. And perhaps belonging is something else altogether. Perhaps one can belong to a place without feeling at home in it. Perhaps one can feel at home in a memory that they can no longer return to. Perhaps one can miss something with all their heart and still be afraid of returning to it.

I do not know yet.

That is, perhaps, the reason I am writing this series in the first place.

I want to understand whether home is somewhere we return to, somewhere we carry with us, or something we spend our lives trying to recreate. I want to understand whether belonging is given to us by a place, by people, or whether it is something we construct within ourselves. I want to understand why some places continue to pull at us long after we have left them, and why, sometimes, the thought of returning can feel just as unsettling as the thought of never returning.

Perhaps, eventually, I will find an answer.

Or perhaps I will simply find better questions
